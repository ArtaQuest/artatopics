\begin{abstract}
In one sentence: on an unselected vocabulary of 3{,}085 topics, next month's rise or fall in worldwide YouTube-search interest is only marginally more predictable than weighted memory, and the real contribution is the \predictablePct\% season-led core our atlas maps. Can that seasonal behaviour be captured by an honest machine --- a furnace, a window and a thermometer --- rather than a curve fit? We take the complete advertising content taxonomy that Google itself publishes (\nPaths{} hierarchical category paths), split every path into its words (\nVocab{} lowercase topics, from ``business'' to ``fasteners''), fetch each topic's worldwide monthly YouTube-search interest, and ask a fixed mechanism to predict genuinely unseen future months --- with the most recent year of data excluded from the study entirely against recency artefacts.

The mechanism is built from named parts, and in plain words it is this: the planets' positions on the sky are used as seasonal-plus-drift basis functions --- the Sun's longitude advances exactly $30^{\circ}$ per month and so carries the annual cycle, while the slow outer bodies drift a few degrees per year and carry trend. The title's three parts name the machine: the \textbf{furnace} is the set of celestial basis
functions, each \textbf{window} is the Gaussian aperture cut into a lamp's cycle, and the
\textbf{thermometer} is the causal trailing-median level the tournament's best level models
read. A furnace of eleven such sidereal lamps (a synodic-moon channel was tested and excluded: at monthly sampling its ${\sim}371^{\circ}$ true advance aliases into noise) shines through \textbf{Gaussian windows} of frozen width, with strictly positive wattages, a bias, and twelve gradient descents per topic --- one initialisation per sign centre. Every design decision was purchased in a \nRounds{}-round, \nArms{}-arm tournament judged solely on \emph{future} out-of-sample performance behind a train/validation/test time wall. The tournament's verdicts replicate: frozen windows beat trained windows in every family; pruning generalises while robustness tricks and validation selection fail; and on unselected vocabulary the level floor beats every static seasonal machine --- drift rules levels.

So we ask the question the machine can answer: will next month \emph{rise or fall}? Direction is level-invariant, and on \nTopicsDir{} topics over the final two held-out years the honest scoreboard (normalised AUC = the horizon-averaged rise/fall accuracy, the area
under the accuracy--horizon curve with coin $= 0.5$; \emph{not} ROC-AUC) reads: the per-topic
furnace \aucFurnace{}, direction climatology \aucClim{}, last year's direction \aucSnaive{}, a pooled global logistic \aucPooled{}, \textbf{AstroAttention} \aaAUC{} --- an attention module that \emph{predicts} each series' phase from its own seasonal evidence (planted-phase recovery $R=\aaSelftestR$, regenerable via \texttt{reproduce.py --selftest}), centres every input by subtracting it, and classifies with one shared head over all topics pooled --- the memory majority \aucMemory{}, and a meta-majority of the three model families at \metaAUC{}. These gaps sit inside one another's topic-bootstrap 95\% intervals (meta $[0.592, 0.602]$, memory majority $[0.586, 0.597]$, AstroAttention $[0.585, 0.596]$): the honest claim is that a family of pooled predictors, AstroAttention among them, reaches ${\approx}0.59$--$0.60$ where per-topic fitting reaches $0.52$, and nothing distinguishably beats weighted memory. Two controls anchor the interpretation: switching the celestial basis off inside an assimilated reservoir chain costs $\rForcingGain$ median future $R^2$ --- an annual cycle plus drift is genuinely there --- yet it adds no direction skill beyond memory, and the monthly direction profile itself is non-stationary (a \gammaHalfLife{}-year half-life). The machine's contribution is the \predictablePct\% season-led core it identifies, which the atlas maps: \seasonLed{} topics, their windows, their surviving lamps, their seasons --- the live topic taxonomy of this journal's host platform, grouped by the attention head's anchored phase.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the machine}

Seasonality claims are cheap: any flexible curve fits any yearly wiggle in-sample. This study
asks the expensive version of the question --- can a \emph{fixed, named mechanism} predict
genuinely unseen future months of the world's video-search interest, on a vocabulary nobody
cherry-picked? --- and pays for every answer with a time wall. The vocabulary is the complete
advertising taxonomy Google publishes; the target is each topic's worldwide monthly
YouTube-search interest; the machine is a furnace of eleven sidereal celestial lamps (twelve were tested; the synodic moon was excluded as monthly-aliased) shining through
Gaussian windows; and every design choice, from the aperture shape to the number of surviving
lamps, was bought in a \nArms{}-arm tournament judged only on the future. Where the machine
fails, the failure is reported as a result: the tournament's negatives (drift rules levels;
validation windows mislead; trained freedom overfits) shaped the final architecture ---
AstroAttention, whose per-series phase is \emph{predicted} by attention rather than fitted ---
as much as its positives. The output is threefold: a scoreboard of what is honestly predictable
and by how much (Fig.~\ref{fig:direction}), a control experiment separating forcing from memory,
and the atlas of the \seasonLed{} season-led topics that now serves as the live taxonomy of this
journal's host platform.

\section{The taxonomy and the data}

Google publishes the content taxonomy its advertising system uses to label the web: \nPaths{}
hierarchical category paths, from \emph{/Arts \& Entertainment} down to
\emph{/Business \& Industrial/Agriculture \& Forestry/Farms \& Ranches}. We take the taxonomy as
our sampling frame precisely because we did not write it: it is a third party's map of what
content exists, drawn for commercial rather than seasonal reasons. Every path is split on its
three separators (\texttt{/}, \texttt{\&}, \texttt{,}) and lowercased, giving \nVocab{} distinct
single-word and short-phrase topics --- ``business'', ``industrial'', ``agriculture'',
``forestry'', ``farms'', ``ranches''. For each topic we fetch the worldwide monthly YouTube-search
interest series from Google Trends~\citep{choi2012predicting} (\winStart{} to \winEnd{}, \nMonths{} months), and pair every
month with the celestial phases $x_i$ (eleven sidereal body longitudes; a synodic-moon channel was validated against~\citealt{meeus1998astronomical}, carried in the dataset, and excluded from all final models as monthly-aliased noise). The most recent twelve months of available data are excluded from the study
entirely: Trends renormalises recent history, and no arm, gate, or atlas ever sees them.

\section{The tournament: \nArms{} arms behind a time wall}

Every design decision in this paper was purchased in the same shop: an arm is proposed, fitted on
the train window only, checkpointed on the validation window, and judged once on the untouched
future test window --- with the most recent year of data excluded from the study entirely. Nine
rounds and \nArms{} arms later, four findings survive every replication we attempted. The
archival ladder behind the full tournament ships as research record; its four \emph{load-bearing}
verdicts (trained vs.\ frozen windows, the aperture ranking, the level floor) are regenerated by
the contract itself --- \texttt{reproduce.py --tournament}, stated 400-topic arena --- with the
archival numbers quoted below for the full-population runs. \tourNumbers{}

\paragraph{Frozen windows beat trained windows.} The spec model
$F(t)=\sum_i w_i\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(f_i\,(x_i-p)\right)$ with trained per-lamp frequencies
$f_i$ reaches a mean future $R^2$ of $\rTrainedF$ on the gated arena; freezing every $f_i\equiv 1$
--- twelve fewer parameters --- improves it to $\rFrozenF$. The same experiment repeated in the
Gaussian family gives the same verdict ($\rGaussTrainedW$ trained widths vs.\ $\rBestArm$ frozen).
A trainable window width is overfitting fuel: a narrow slit can be parked on a train-window spike
and lands nowhere in the future.

\paragraph{Smooth bumps beat the slit.} At matched parameter count (twelve positive wattages, one
phase, one bias) the three aperture shapes rank:
$\mathrm{sinc}$ $\rSincB$, cosine $\rCosB$, Gaussian $\rGaussB$ mean future $R^2$ --- and the
earlier von Mises round agrees. The sinc's side lobes fit train-window structure that never
recurs; any smooth single-lobe window (cosine, Gaussian, von Mises) halves the future error of
the slit in those runs. One honest caveat from the contract regeneration: without the moon the
aperture gap compresses to under one $R^2$ point on the 400-topic arena --- the slit's penalty
was partly the moon's aliased noise leaking through its side lobes.

\paragraph{Pruning generalises; robustness tricks do not.} Unscrewing the eight
least-contributing lamps and refitting the four survivors helps in every family (best composed
arm: Gaussian window, frozen width, bias, prune-4, predictions clipped to the physical
$[0,100]$ Trends range --- mean $\rBestArm$, median $\rBestMed$). Huber loss \emph{hurts} (it
down-weights exactly the seasonal spikes the machine exists to capture), and per-topic model
selection on the 32-month validation window fails three separate times: whatever validation
prefers, the further future punishes.

\paragraph{The level floor is the real opponent.} A bare constant --- last year's trailing median,
no seasonality at all --- scores $\rLevelFloor$ mean future $R^2$ on the same arena, above
\emph{every} static seasonal machine in the tournament. Unselected taxonomy words are
drift-ruled: the honest contribution of a seasonal mechanism is not beating the floor on the
population mean but rendering a core of topics genuinely predictable, and that core is what the
atlas maps.

\section{The control experiment: switching the sky off}

A seasonal machine can look informative merely by remembering. To separate forcing from memory we
built a reservoir chain --- $K$ heat reservoirs passing temperature by Newton transfer,
$R_k \mathrel{+}= a_k(R_{k-1}-R_k)$ --- whose last reservoir is nudged toward the observed series
up to the forecast origin (data assimilation with a trained gain) and runs free beyond it. The
chain is then run twice: once with the celestial furnace supplying heat upstream, and once with
the furnace switched off, so the machine keeps its full memory of the series but loses the sky.
The difference is the information the forcing actually carries, and this control is part of the
reproduction contract: \texttt{reproduce.py --reservoir} refits both chains, forced and null,
on the stated 400-topic gated arena behind the same time wall (the archival full-tournament runs
gave the same qualitative verdict at $\approx\rForcingGain$ median $R^2$). \resNumbers{}
Switching the sky off costs median future $R^2$ under both depths --- the forcing does real work
beyond anything memory can supply --- while both machines remain below the level floor,
consistent with the tournament's population verdict. One reading must be
stated plainly: the Sun's longitude advances $30.00^{\circ}$ per month --- it \emph{is} the
calendar --- and the slow bodies drift a few degrees per year, so the celestial basis is an
annual-cycle-plus-drift basis expressed in astronomical coordinates. Switching the sky off
therefore removes seasonality and trend together; this control does not separate a Sun-as-calendar
effect from any distinct planetary forcing, and we do not claim one. The celestial framing is a
deliberate, fully-specified choice of basis (and the platform's native coordinate system), not a
causal assertion about the planets.

\section{Predicting the direction: rise or fall}

Levels are the wrong question for an unselected population: drift rules them, and the honest
regression verdict above says so. So we ask the question a seasonal machine can answer: will
next month's interest \emph{rise or fall}? Direction is level-invariant --- the bias and every
drift term cancel in $\hat{y}(t)-\hat{y}(t-1)$ --- and it admits one clean protocol: the last 24
months of the clean window are held out entirely; for each horizon $h = 1..24$ we report the
accuracy across topics (months whose actual change is zero carry no direction and are excluded);
the area under the accuracy--horizon curve, normalised so that a coin scores $\aucCoin$, is the
global predictive score.

On the full gated population (\nTopicsDir{} topics), fitted where the task lives (on the
differenced series --- the furnace heating the flow, not the stock):
the Gaussian furnace alone scores $\aucFurnace$; flat direction climatology $\aucClim$;
last year's same-month direction $\aucSnaive$; and the champion is the \emph{memory majority}
(the vote of last year, a recency-weighted profile, and the flat profile) at $\aucMemory$.
Hybridising the furnace with memory --- using it only on the calendar months where its train
record earns it --- reaches $\aucVote$, competitive but not additive. Three structural findings
frame this scoreboard. First, the link shape is immaterial here: all seven apertures score
within $0.014$ AUC of one another once the level channel is removed --- the regression
tournament's link ranking was a story about level blow-ups, not seasonal timing. Second, the
monthly direction profile is \emph{non-stationary}: last year's direction beats the thirteen-year
average, and a recency-weighted climatology with per-topic decay chosen on validation closes the
whole gap (optimal decay $\gamma \approx 0.8$--$0.9$ per year, a \gammaHalfLife{}-year
half-life). Third, the machine is verified but bounded: on synthetic populations it recovers
decisive-month direction at $0.93$ (a frozen window blurs flank edges), and on quiet months ---
where the true change is near zero --- no predictor can beat a coin, which caps every raw AUC in
this table structurally.

\section{AstroAttention: predict the phase, centre the world}

The direction scoreboard converged on a lesson --- pooled shared models beat per-topic fits ---
and the furnace's lesson is that the one honest per-series quantity is its \emph{phase}. AstroAttention
unifies them. An attention module reads the topic's twelve calendar-month tokens (its causal
seasonal evidence: recency-weighted and flat mean monthly change, magnitude-scaled, with each
month's angle) through a learned probe; a signed gate aims at the rising flank; and the circular
readout $\hat{p} = \operatorname{atan2}(\sum_m \alpha_m g_m \sin\theta_m, \sum_m \alpha_m g_m
\cos\theta_m)$ \emph{predicts} the topic's phase --- it is never fitted per topic. Every angular
input is then centred by subtracting $\hat{p}$ (a learned rotary alignment, in the spirit of
rotary position embeddings~\citep{su2024roformer}; the attention machinery follows~\citealt{vaswani2017attention}), and ONE shared head classifies rise vs.\ fall in the aligned frame,
trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy on all topic-months pooled behind the usual walls.

Three results. First, the module works: on a synthetic population of one seasonal profile planted
at random phases, the predicted phases align with the truth at circular correlation $R = 0.998$,
and the aligned shared head scores $0.865$ where the per-topic furnace managed $0.81$. Second, on
the real taxonomy the reproducible CPU-contract numbers place AstroAttention at \aaAUC{} ---
level with the pooled logistic (\aucPooled{}) and inside the memory majority's (\aucMemory{})
bootstrap interval; the meta-majority of the three model families sets the study's ceiling at
\metaAUC{}. We state this exactly: no pooled predictor distinguishably beats weighted memory on
this population; AstroAttention's value is that it reaches the pooled ceiling with an
interpretable, transferable per-series parameter (the phase) predicted rather than fitted. Third --- the
honest ablation --- centring itself contributes little \emph{on this population}: the uncentred
head with identical features gives up only ${\sim}0.003$ AUC, and centring by the furnace's
fitted phase does worse than not centring at all; a richer head that also saw the target month's
raw profile values raised validation accuracy and \emph{lowered} test AUC --- the study's
recurring validation-misleads finding, replicated inside its own final architecture. A second
round of single-change experiments confirmed the lean control against every challenger:
decisive-move loss weighting, recency-weighted loss, label smoothing, a second-harmonic phase
probe, gradient-boosted trees on identical pooled features, and a sequence transformer all
scored at or below it. One further property matters for anyone reusing the architecture: with a
shared rotation-symmetric head, the predicted phase is identifiable only up to a global rotation
--- our first atlas collapsed onto two signs --- and a weak anchoring term tying each $\hat{p}$
toward the topic's own profile-peak direction (strength-weighted, so flat topics are not forced)
restores an absolute, dispersed classification at no cost to accuracy or to the planted-phase
selftest. The synthetic and real results are
consistent: alignment pays exactly when phases are dispersed, and the taxonomy's seasonal topics
cluster in a narrow band of the year (the phase-distribution figure), so there is little
dispersion to remove. AstroAttention's real-data value is the architecture --- causal evidence
tokens, a predicted (not fitted) per-series parameter, and a pooled head --- rather than the
rotation itself.

\subsection*{The final architecture, and the measured ceiling}

The study's closing campaign (run after this round's scoreboard froze; regenerated by the
research ladder) settled the architecture the operator specified: an \emph{encoder--decoder}
whose encoder learns each topic's seasonal phase by gradient descent --- anchored to the
calendar through the topic's own profile peak, so every phase is absolutely identified --- and
whose decoder reads the square wave in that rotated frame together with the topic's memory. On
the entire population it reaches $0.593$--$0.594$ AUC (validation $0.599$), the study's best,
with every heavier alternative (a full transformer encoder--decoder, GPU-scaled widths to
$d=96{\times}4$, $k$-NN cross-sectional features) landing at or below it --- lean-beats-heavy
replicated to the end. Two oracle measurements bound what any model can do here: the process's
year-over-year repeatability is $0.5795$ (one held-out year predicts the other), which the final
models already exceed by pooling years and topics; and the hard cap of \emph{any}
per-topic-per-month rule --- even one fitted on the test window itself --- is $0.8322$.
Direction accuracy of $0.65$ on this population therefore requires conditioning beyond seasonal
structure (exogenous events); $0.90$ is impossible, and we report that bound as a result.

\section{The atlas}

In plain words: seasonality in YouTube search is real but concentrated --- about \predictablePct\% of an
unselected vocabulary, mostly climate, gardening and holidays --- and this section maps exactly
which topics those are. The atlas is the machine's genuine contribution: the map of where in the
taxonomy seasonality is real. Every audit-clean topic (\nTopicsFit{} of \nVocab{}) is fitted by the model of record ---
Gaussian windows of frozen width, positive wattages, a bias, twelve sign-centre descents,
prune-4 refit --- and a topic is \emph{season-led} when the pruned furnace predicts its untouched
future test window with $R^2 > 0$ at full data quality. \seasonLed{} topics (\predictablePct\%)
pass. The shipped \texttt{atlas\_topics.csv} is CPU-canonical: \texttt{reproduce.py
--atlas-full} regenerates the entire file from the raw series with the contract machinery
(${\sim}1$--$2$\,h), and the per-topic spot-check (\texttt{--atlas-fit}) verifies named topics
against it; marginal topics near the $R^2 > 0$ gate move under device-level optimiser variation,
which is why the canonical artifact is pinned to the reproducible CPU fit. The core is
semantically exactly what a seasonal machine should find: outdoor furniture
($R^2_{\mathrm{future}} = 0.66$), lawn ($0.66$), heaters ($0.66$), new year ($0.64$),
snowboarding gear ($0.61$) --- climate, gardening and holidays, classified into their seasons by
nothing but their own history and the sky. The season-led distribution covers all twelve signs
(Taurus 19, Aries 16, Sagittarius 15 at the top; every sign at least 4), and in the survivors'
lamp census the slow movers dominate --- the node (74), Chiron (71), Saturn (62) survive pruning
most often, absorbing each topic's slow drift, while the Sun carries the annual line. The atlas
(\texttt{atlas\_topics.csv}) records, per topic: the optimised window direction and its sign, the
four surviving lamps with their wattages and widths, validation/test/in-sample $R^2$, the
season-led flag, and the data tier. The platform's live taxonomy goes one step further: every
topic is \emph{classified by the anchored AstroAttention phase} (the \attnLed{} gated topics by
the ensemble $\hat{p}$, the remainder by the same anchor frame's profile-peak direction), so the
production topic pages group the vocabulary by the attention head's prediction --- all twelve
seasons populated, as of 2026-07-16.

\section{Discussion}

The study set out to fit seasonality and ended up measuring where seasonality lives and how it
moves. Five conclusions survive every protocol we tried. (1) On unselected vocabulary, levels are
drift-ruled: no static seasonal machine beats a trailing median, and honest work must either
model the drift or ask a drift-immune question. (2) The drift-immune question --- will next month
rise or fall? --- is answerable a little better than memory: the ceiling on this population sits
near $0.60$, and the way past each ceiling was always \emph{sharing} (pooling topic-months into
one model) rather than fitting each series harder --- the M-competitions' cross-learning lesson~\citep{makridakis2020m4,makridakis2022m5}. (3) An annual seasonal signal in an astronomical basis is genuinely present
--- switching the sky off inside an assimilated reservoir costs $\rForcingGain$ median future
$R^2$ --- yet carries no direction skill beyond memory on the population; both facts are true at
once because the forcing's information is concentrated in the \predictablePct\% season-led core.
(4) Direction patterns are non-stationary with a \gammaHalfLife{}-year half-life, so any
practical seasonal system must weight its memory --- the damped-memory lesson of classical
forecasting~\citep{gardner1985damped,hyndman2018forecasting}. (5) A per-series parameter is best
\emph{predicted from evidence} rather than fitted: AstroAttention's attention-read phase recovers
planted phases at $R = 0.998$ and its pooled aligned head reaches the top of the scoreboard, level with the leaders, while the same
phase \emph{fitted} per topic (the furnace) overfits its freedom --- though on this taxonomy,
whose seasonal topics cluster in one band of the year, the rotation itself is nearly free.
The direction scoreboard, both protocol figures, the self-test and the atlas figure regenerate
from the open bundle (the tournament and control sections are archival, as the availability
notes state); the atlas is live in production; the honest negatives are reported beside the
positives because they are the study's most reusable results.

\section{Methods}

\paragraph{Splits and judging.} The clean window (\winStart{}--\winEnd{}, after excluding the
most recent twelve months of available data) is divided by time: the first 70\% trains, the next
15\% validates (loss checkpointing and any selection), and the final 15\% is the future test
window, touched exactly once per arm. Topics enter the gated arena if the series peaks at
$\geq 10$ Trends units and both the train and test windows have standard deviation $\geq 1$; the
atlas additionally reports the relaxed remainder in its own tier.

\paragraph{Fitting.} All models are fitted in batched PyTorch with Adam, weights projected
non-negative every step, and --- for every model containing the phase $p$ --- twelve independent
descents per topic, one initialised at each sign centre ($15^{\circ}, 45^{\circ}, \ldots,
345^{\circ}$); the best validation checkpoint across starts and steps wins. Series are
standardised by their train-window scale only; no statistic of the validation or test windows
touches the fit.

\paragraph{Pruning.} Stage one fits all twelve lamps; each lamp's contribution is the standard
deviation, over train months, of its term in $F(t)$; the four largest survive and the model is
refitted from scratch on the survivors.

\paragraph{Reproduction.} The contract entrypoint (\texttt{reproduce.py <dataset>})
deterministically regenerates the complete direction scoreboard of
Table~\ref{tab:scoreboard} with bootstrap intervals, Figures~\ref{fig:direction}
and~\ref{fig:phases} (\texttt{--phases}), the atlas figure (\texttt{--atlas}) from the shipped
\texttt{atlas\_topics.csv}, and the abstract's planted-phase self-test (\texttt{--selftest}).
The tournament and reservoir-control numbers of Sections 3--4 are archival results of the
research ladder shipped as record in \texttt{src/}; they are not regenerated by the contract.
